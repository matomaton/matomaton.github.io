The traveler's goal, and where it breaks

One simple goal, and three steps to see why we are not meeting it.

1

The goal

"Show me where I can go on my dates, so I can pick one and book it."

2

What happens

The answer depends on which door they use. The resort list, the booking bar and the chatbot return different sets of resorts for the same trip.

3

Why it hurts

Nothing tells them a resort is missing, so they assume the list is complete. They book from a smaller set of options, or leave. One research participant caught it, but only by running three searches.

Production chatbot logs (about 27,000 sessions, Jun 29 to Aug 31) show the same pattern, including Orlando-scoped answers that omit Mystic Dunes while a broader search shows it available.

Why the chatbot case is the worst one

On the site: silent omission

Two paths quietly disagree. The traveler assumes the list is the full picture and either books from incomplete options or leaves.

In chat: assert, then deny

The assistant plans around a resort, denies it when asked, then drops a property it had just called the only verified option. A production session shows the same thing: "It's on your web page, Dunkeld Scotland."

Search team

Itinerary Builder and Plan a Trip

Design constraints for the sprint

Whatever we redesign in find and book inherits this trust gap unless the new flow treats these as acceptance criteria.

  1. One trip question, one inventory answerExplore, the booking bar, Plan a Trip and Itinerary Builder candidates should agree for the same inputs.
  2. If a list is scoped or filtered, say so"Showing Orlando city only" beats silent incompleteness, which reads as "that's all there is."
  3. Area language matches traveler geography"Orlando area" that excludes Celebration feels broken. Offer a one click "include nearby."
  4. Chat must not plan on inventory it later deniesItinerary plans inherit the error, and the traveler cannot book from them.
  5. Never bury real destination inventory under off-destination recommendationsRecommendations are fine. Substitution, like Las Vegas inside a Scotland search, is not.

What the sprint can design, and what it cannot

The sprint can design

  • A visible scope line and "not shown" explanation on results
  • An always available "All resorts for this destination" view
  • A clearly labeled, separate recommendations block
  • Available and bookable resorts ranked first
  • How a chat or plan recovers when its own inventory changes

Needs Search and engineering

  • Whether the list page and booking bar call the same service with the same parameters
  • Which inventory tools the chatbot calls on each turn
  • Whether exact City, State matching is a rule or an enhancement
  • Why four Scotland resorts and Mystic Dunes were not returned
